## The Adoption Picture

Artificial intelligence is no longer a niche technology. OECD data show firm-level AI adoption more than doubled from 8.7 percent in 2023 to 20.2 percent in 2025, driven largely by generative AI becoming widely available. Adoption is highest in knowledge-intensive sectors — ICT reached 57.3 percent — but the fastest recent growth is coming from sectors that started low, such as accommodation and food services and construction.

There is also a clear firm-size gap. Large firms reached roughly 52 percent adoption in 2025, while small firms stood at about 17.4 percent. The absolute gap has widened, which suggests that established routines and capability, not just resources, shape how quickly firms adopt AI.

## What It Means for Business

The productivity evidence is positive but uneven. Studies put firm-level productivity gains at roughly 4 to 15 percent, yet 56 percent of CEOs in PwC's latest global survey reported zero measurable AI return on investment. A small group of high performers captures outsized returns, while most organisations are still in the expensive restructuring phase.

The audit profession is also being reshaped. EY's Helix platform now analyses 100 percent of client journal entries, and the other Big Four firms have made large, publicly announced AI investments. Regulators, including the FRC and PCAOB, are watching whether audit quality controls keep pace.

## The Accounting Angle

AI is becoming visible in corporate filings: the share of S&P 500 companies disclosing AI-related risks jumped from 12 percent in 2023 to 72 percent in 2025. With the EU AI Act now in force and AI-related securities litigation rising, disclosure quality is becoming a material governance question.
