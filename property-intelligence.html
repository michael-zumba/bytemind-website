Why we built it

A property tool, built like a research project.

ByteMind is a small analytics practice. Property decisions are often made with a number nobody can inspect. No inputs, no error, no date.

We built this engine to be inspectable. Public listing data and council valuations go in. The method is one we use in research. Compare like with like. Hold data back. Measure the error. Write down the limits. Every range names the signal it came from.

We also show where the method is weak. Text analysis helped when the council valuation was missing. It did not improve the full model, and no single topic survived correction. A reader can see the difference between a strong signal and a weak one.

It is decision support, not a valuation and not advice. But if a number is going to be repeated in a client meeting, it should arrive with its source and its error. That is the standard we hold this tool to.

The commercial problem

A single number is a sales device. A decision needs a range.

Automated property estimates are now part of how New Zealand property is discussed, priced and marketed. The pattern that comes with them is what gets in the way of a decision.

01

One number, no error bar

A point estimate cannot tell you whether a $40,000 gap between two homes is signal or noise. Without a stated error, a precise-looking figure invites a decision it cannot support.

02

Opaque by construction

When the inputs, weights and training sample are not visible, you cannot tell whether a value reflects the property or the model's habit. That matters when the number is about to be quoted to a vendor or a lender.

03

Coverage that quietly thins out

Automated estimates cluster where data is easiest: standard freehold houses. New builds, units, cross-lease and leasehold titles often fall outside the model, and that gap is rarely stated up front.

04

Stale by the time it matters

A market that reprices weekly, described by a median refreshed quarterly, is a lagging indicator dressed as a current one. The date behind a number is part of the number.

This is a property of the category, not a charge against any particular vendor: point-estimate automation is the dominant design because it is easy to sell. A decision-maker's requirement is different — a range, its source, its error and its date — and that is what this page describes.

The approach

Four commitments that change what you can do with the number.

The point is not to be more precise. It is to be honest enough that the output can carry a decision.

01 · Calibration

Ranges with stated error

Every value is a range. The calibrated fallback model publishes its error and an empirical 80% interval fitted on out-of-fold residuals; the no-CV variant is labelled as weaker, not hidden.

In practice: you can size a negotiation buffer instead of anchoring on a false precision.
02 · Provenance

Every number source-attributed

Homes.co.nz AVM, council CV, sold comparables, suburb guide or ByteMind's own hedonic fallback: the app states which signal produced each range, and when that signal was published.

In practice: you can see which part of the evidence is third-party and which part is ByteMind's model.
03 · Depth

Micro-market depth

17 North Shore and Eastern Bays suburbs, each with its own median range, CV ratio, days on market and AVM coverage — not a single Auckland-wide median applied to every street.

In practice: you can compare like with like, and see when a suburb's evidence is thin.
04 · Audit

Auditable method, daily refresh

The pipeline runs daily at 03:15, keeps the inputs behind each range for audit, and publishes a static snapshot. The methodology is written down, including what the model cannot do.

In practice: the number you saw yesterday can be explained today, and checked.

Method

Five steps from a listing page to a value range.

The model never presents a single "true value". It chooses the best available signal for each property, labels it, and says what it cannot support.

  1. Recover the price signal
  2. Normalise by council CV
  3. Independent comp model
  4. Calibrated fallback
  5. Review & limitations

  1. 01 Recover the price signal

    For most listings the public page embeds a Homes.co.nz estimate range, the council capital value with its land/improvement split, and up to ten nearby sales. Those values are extracted as published, with their source and update date.

    Guardrail: if a page carries no usable signal, nothing is invented to fill the gap.

  2. 02 Normalise by council CV

    Council CVs are not market values, but they are a common denominator. Dividing a value by CV lets a 90 m² unit and an 800 m² house be compared on the same basis instead of on raw price.

    Guardrail: the CV ratio is shown as a cross-check, never as a market value.

  3. 03 Independent comparable model

    Nearby sold prices are weighted by distance, recency, property type and same-suburb, then normalised by CV. The weighted 25th–75th percentile forms the range; confidence follows effective sample size and dispersion.

    Guardrail: the comparable pool is small — — de-duplicated sales in the current snapshot — so wide ranges are reported as wide.

  4. 04 Calibrated hedonic fallback

    For properties no Homes AVM matches, a five-fold-validated hedonic model predicts a range from CV, land and floor area, build decade, tenure, suburb and sale method. Its 80% interval is calibrated on out-of-fold residuals.

    Guardrail: it is labelled a fallback, never presented as a Homes AVM. Where no CV exists the weaker no-CV variant is used and stated as such.

  5. 05 Review and publish the limits

    Quality checks, tenure flags (leasehold, cross-lease, unit title) and a daily snapshot sit on top of the models. Limitations are published with the tool rather than buried in a method appendix.

    Guardrail: leasehold and cross-lease are detected from listing text, not title records — verify the title and ground rent before any offer.

What this is — and what it is not

  • Aggregate view only, on this site. This page presents aggregate market indicators and method. It does not re-publish listing-level content; the listing-level research interface is hosted separately on the independent research site.
  • Decision support, not a valuation. ByteMind Property Intelligence is an analytical tool. It is not a valuation under professional standards and is not a substitute for a registered valuation where one is required, for example for lending, legal or insurance purposes.
  • Ranges, not promises. Every automated value carries error. The calibrated fallback's error is stated; third-party estimates carry their own, and no figure on this page is a forecast or a guarantee of return. Nothing here is financial, legal or tax advice.
  • Third-party data stays third-party. ByteMind does not redistribute third-party data. Source estimates are attributed to their provider and shown as published; the model, method and audit trail are ByteMind's own.
  • A snapshot, not a live feed. Figures refresh daily. Verify the title, any ground rent, and consented works before making an offer.
