## Summary

People have strong opinions about New Zealand's housing market, but several widely held beliefs do not hold up against the data. Overseas buyers were never a major force behind national house prices. First-home buyers are buying more homes than ever, not fewer, reaching a record 27.5 percent of all purchases in 2025. And the lending rules introduced since 2021 mean that even with mortgage rates falling, prices are not taking off again.

## Key Findings

**First-home buyers at record levels.** Their share of purchases rose from about 21 percent (2017-2020) to 27.5 percent in 2025. About 24,800 properties were bought by first-home buyers in the twelve months to early 2026. They paid a median price of $700,000, roughly $87,000 below the national median.

**Overseas buyers were never a big factor nationally.** At their peak in March 2018, overseas buyers made up 3.3 percent of home transfers. The foreign buyer ban (October 2018) cut this to 0.3 to 0.4 percent. National house prices then rose 65 percent between December 2018 and November 2021, with overseas buyers no longer in the market.

**Prices tell different stories across the country.** Auckland (down about 22 percent) and Wellington (down about 26 percent) remain well below their 2021 peaks. Canterbury and Southland have set new records.

**Lending rules have changed how the market works.** Debt-to-income limits introduced in July 2024, alongside existing deposit rules, mean that even when interest rates fall, buyers cannot borrow as much as they could before. This is a key reason why steep OCR cuts (from 5.50 percent to 2.25 percent) did not set off another price boom.
