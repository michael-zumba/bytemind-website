## A New Asset Class

AI tokens grant holders access to decentralised AI computation, model inference, and data services. The sector grew from a negligible USD 0.2 billion in early 2020 to roughly USD 25 billion at its peak in early 2026 — faster, and with more volatility, than the broader crypto market.

Institutional interest is real. Grayscale has launched a Decentralized AI Fund, and venture funding into AI token projects reached USD 4.9 billion in a single quarter in 2025. But the category's boundaries are contested, and market data quality carries the usual crypto caveats.

## Accounting and Risk

FASB ASU 2023-08 replaces the old impairment-only model with fair value measurement for crypto assets, which fundamentally changes how AI token holdings appear in financial statements. Under IFRS, the treatment is still unsettled, and the IASB added digital assets to its research agenda.

Risk is also material: annualised volatility for major AI tokens ranges from about 85 to 110 percent, compared with roughly 15 percent for the S&P 500. That matters for corporate treasuries, margin requirements, and risk-weighted capital.

## What to Watch

The central open question is whether tokens held to access computational services should be classified differently from tokens held purely for investment. That question remains unresolved under both US GAAP and IFRS.
