The project

Built like a research project.

ByteMind is a small analytics practice. Property decisions are often made with a number nobody can inspect: no inputs, no error, no date.

We built this engine to be inspectable. Public listing data and council valuations go in. The method is one we use in research: compare like with like, hold data back, measure the error, and write down the limits. Every range names the signal it came from.

We also show where it is weak. Text analysis helped when the council valuation was missing, but no topic survived correction, so it is not used in the valuation. That is how a reader can tell a strong signal from a weak one.

Method

From a listing page to a value range.

Each range chooses the best available signal, labels it, and says what it cannot support.

  1. 01 Recover the price signal

    Asking price, guide, deadline or auction date, read from the listing page and dated.

  2. 02 Value the tenure

    Leasehold, cross-lease and unit title are valued differently before any number is produced.

  3. 03 Compare nearby sales

    Sold prices weighted by distance and recency, normalised by council capital value where one is available.

  4. 04 Calibrate a fallback

    A hedonic model for listings without an AVM or usable comparables, with an 80% interval and its error published.

Scope

Scope and limits.

An independent research project, not a commercial service. No fees, engagements or advisory offer is made through this page.

What this is — and what it is not

  • Market-level view with on-demand summaries. Click a map bubble for a single-listing summary; the full results list, listing interface and CSV live on the independent research site.
  • Decision support, not a valuation. Not a valuation under professional standards, not a substitute for a registered valuation, and not financial, legal or tax advice.
  • A snapshot, not a live feed. Figures refresh daily. Verify the title, any ground rent and consented works before making an offer.
