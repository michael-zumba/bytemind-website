The key difference between zero-rated and exempt supplies in New Zealand GST lies in their technical classification and the resulting ability to claim back GST on expenses. Both result in you charging your customer 0% or no GST, but only zero-rated supplies are considered "taxable supplies" and allow you to claim input tax credits.

Here is a breakdown of the differences, with examples and tax consequences.

### The Core Difference

While you do not collect any GST from your customer in either case, the fundamental difference is how they are treated within the GST system.

* **Zero-rated supplies** are a type of **taxable supply**. The law applies a 0% GST rate to them. Because they are taxable, you can still claim back the GST you paid on your own business expenses (input tax) related to making those supplies.
* **Exempt supplies** are **not taxable supplies**. They are entirely outside the GST system. This means you do not charge GST on them, and crucially, **you cannot claim GST credits** for any expenses you incur in making those exempt supplies.

### Examples of Zero-Rated and Exempt Supplies

To make this clearer, here are common examples for each type of supply, sourced from Inland Revenue (IRD).

| Category | Zero-Rated Supplies (0% GST) | Exempt Supplies (No GST) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Common Examples** | • **Exported goods**: Selling goods to an overseas customer who will take them out of New Zealand.<br>• **Most exported services**: A New Zealand architect designing a building for an overseas client.<br>• **Sale of a going concern**: Selling your entire business as a going concern to another GST-registered person.<br>• **Zero-rated land transactions**: Selling land between two GST-registered persons where the buyer intends to use it for taxable activity. | • **Residential rent**: Renting out a residential dwelling.<br>• **Financial services**: Bank fees, interest on loans, and charges for exchanging currency.<br>• **Penalty interest**: Interest charged on overdue accounts.<br>• **Donated goods sold by non-profits**: A charity selling donated second-hand goods. |
| **Special Case: Fine Metals** | • **Initial supply by refiner**: When a **refiner** sells newly-refined fine metal (e.g., 99.5% pure gold) to a **dealer** as an investment item, it is **zero-rated**. | • **Supply by a dealer**: When a **dealer** subsequently sells that fine metal, the supply is **exempt**. |

### Tax Consequences: The Right to Claim GST Back

The most important practical difference is the impact on your GST return and your ability to claim back GST on your costs.

* **For Zero-Rated Supplies**: Because these are taxable supplies, you are still carrying out a taxable activity. This means you can claim input tax credits for the GST included in your costs (e.g., materials, professional fees, stock) related to that zero-rated supply. This often results in a refund from the IRD, as you have GST costs but no GST income.
* **For Exempt Supplies**: As these are not taxable, you are not considered to be carrying out a taxable activity in relation to them. Therefore, **you cannot claim any GST input tax credits** for expenses related to your exempt supplies. If you make both exempt and taxable supplies, you must apportion your expenses to claim GST only on the portion related to your taxable (including zero-rated) supplies.
