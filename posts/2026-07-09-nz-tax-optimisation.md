## Summary

New Zealand's tax system is simple by international standards: low rates, few loopholes, no general capital gains tax, no stamp duty, no payroll tax, and no tax-free threshold. Because of this, cutting tax legitimately is rarely about clever structures. It comes down to three practical habits: choosing the right business structure, claiming every deduction you are genuinely entitled to, and paying on time so you avoid interest and penalties.

## Key Points

**Shifting income around saves less than it used to.** Since the top personal tax rate and the trust tax rate were both set at 39 percent (from the 2024/25 tax year), putting income into a trust no longer lowers the rate. The company rate of 28 percent is still below the top personal rate, but for an owner on 39 percent that is only a delay, not a real saving: when the company pays the profit out as a dividend, extra tax tops it up to 39 percent.

**Deductions add up to the biggest saving.** For most small businesses, the largest reliable savings come from claiming every legitimate expense and keeping good records. That includes the right share of home-office costs, the correct method for vehicle costs, the rule that most business entertainment is only 50 percent deductible, and the Budget 2025 Investment Boost (a 20 percent upfront deduction on eligible new assets first used on or after 22 May 2025).

**Pay provisional tax on time to avoid interest.** Provisional tax means paying next year's tax in instalments. If your prior-year tax bill was under $60,000 and you pay the standard amounts on time, you are protected from interest charges even if you end up owing a bit more. That matters because the interest rate on underpaid tax is 8.97 percent from 16 January 2026.

**Use the incentives the government provides.** A few reliefs are deliberately offered and often missed: the 28 percent cap on tax for managed-fund (PIE) income, the yearly KiwiSaver government contribution, and the 15 percent Research and Development Tax Incentive.

## The Boundary

All of these steps are legitimate only when they reflect what is genuinely happening in your business and personal life. The general anti-avoidance rule (sections BG 1 and GA 1 of the Income Tax Act 2007) lets Inland Revenue cancel arrangements whose main purpose is simply to avoid tax. The safe approach is substance: pay salaries for real work, give shares to real owners, and only pay family members for work they actually do.
