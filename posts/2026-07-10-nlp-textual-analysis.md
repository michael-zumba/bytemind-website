## The Field in Numbers

Textual analysis has become one of the most active empirical programmes in accounting and finance. Publication counts rose from about two papers per year in 2005 to roughly ninety in 2023, with the fastest acceleration after 2017 as machine learning entered the mainstream. Accounting journals publish about 61 percent of the work, finance journals about 31 percent.

## Methods Have Changed the Game

The methodological journey runs from general-purpose dictionaries to domain-specific word lists, machine learning classifiers, word embeddings, transformers, and now large language models. The jump in accuracy is substantial: FinBERT reaches sentiment classification accuracy near 88 percent, compared with roughly 62 percent for dictionary baselines.

The content of the findings is consistent across methods: disclosure tone predicts returns and future performance, readability shapes how markets process information, and textual signals carry information beyond the numbers.

## Where the Research Is Going

Generative models are both research tools and objects of study. They lower the barrier to entry, but they also raise concerns about prompt sensitivity, reproducibility, and look-ahead bias. The report argues for out-of-sample discipline, transparent reporting, and human-coded benchmarks as the field matures.
