# Why I Bought a Lotto Ticket This Week (Despite Knowing the Maths)

Wednesday's Powerball sits at $35 million, the largest jackpot this year. A single line gives you odds of 1 in 38,383,800. As an accounting academic, I am comfortable with numbers. I ran the calculation. I still bought a ticket.

The published odds answer a question nobody asks: what is the chance *this specific combination* is drawn? The question running through my head at the dairy counter is different: someone wins eventually. Could it be me?

That shift changes the answer from 1 in 38.4 million to about 1 in 2.4 million (exact: 1 in 2,420,000, derived by conditioning on a winner existing among ~2.5 million lines sold per draw). It is not better odds. It is a different question. Bayes' theorem at $6.00.

The ticket also buys 48 hours of anticipatory utility (Loewenstein, 1987), an asymmetric payoff Taleb would recognise, and a low-stakes prompt for the kind of strategic thinking that psychological distance research says we rarely make time for.
