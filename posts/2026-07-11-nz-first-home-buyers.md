## Summary

The New Zealand property market is the subject of an unusually passionate public debate, but several widely held assumptions do not survive contact with the data. Overseas buyers were never a material driver of national house prices. First-home buyers have not been squeezed out; they have increased their market share to a record 27.5 percent. And risk in the housing finance system is being actively managed through an LVR-DTI dual macroprudential framework unprecedented in New Zealand's history.

## Key Findings

**First-home buyer activity at record levels.** FHB share of purchases rose from approximately 21 percent (2017-2020) to 27.5 percent in 2025, with quarterly records set repeatedly. Approximately 24,800 properties were purchased by FHBs in the year to Q3 2025. The median FHB purchase price of $700,000 sits approximately $87,000 below the national median.

**Overseas buyers were never a material national driver.** At their peak in March 2018, overseas buyers accounted for 3.3 percent of home transfers nationally. The foreign buyer ban (October 2018) reduced this to 0.3-0.4 percent. National house prices then rose 65 percent from December 2018 to November 2021, with overseas buyers absent from the market.

**Regional divergence is stark.** Auckland (-22 percent) and Wellington (-26 percent) remain well below their 2021 HPI peaks. Canterbury and Southland have set new records. The national flat trend conceals this split.

**Macroprudential policy has structurally changed.** The activation of DTI restrictions (July 2024) alongside LVR limits creates a dual framework designed to break the interest-rate-to-house-price transmission channel. This helps explain why aggressive OCR cuts (5.50 percent to 2.25 percent) have not reignited price growth.
