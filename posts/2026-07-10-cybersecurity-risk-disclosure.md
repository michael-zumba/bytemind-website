## The New Disclosure Regime

In July 2023, the SEC adopted rules requiring listed companies to disclose material cybersecurity incidents within four business days and to describe their cyber risk management and board oversight in annual reports. The incident-reporting requirement took effect in December 2023, and early data show roughly half of companies made their first filing within the four-business-day window.

The rules sit alongside a broader global shift. The EU's NIS2 and DORA frameworks impose their own incident reporting timelines, including an initial notification within four hours under DORA. For multinational firms, compliance is now a multi-jurisdiction exercise.

## What the Data Show

The first year produced a clear pattern: materiality is the hardest judgment. Many initial filings characterise the material impact as undetermined, and amendments are common. The SEC has also signalled enforcement intent, filing its first actions under the new rules and charging the first individual CISO with fraud.

## Why It Matters

Cyber incidents carry real market consequences — successful attacks are associated with substantial shareholder losses — and the new disclosure regime creates a standardised panel of data that researchers can use to study risk communication, governance, and market reactions.
