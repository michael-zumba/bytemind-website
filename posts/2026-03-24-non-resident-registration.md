One of the more nuanced areas of New Zealand tax law involves overseas businesses that incur expenses in New Zealand but do not make any sales to New Zealand customers. 

Normally, a business only registers for GST if it is making taxable supplies (sales) in New Zealand. However, under **Section 54B** of the Goods and Services Tax Act 1985, a special rule exists that allows certain non-resident businesses to register for GST.

### The Purpose of Section 54B

Consider an overseas company that spends money in New Zealand—perhaps hiring a venue, purchasing local equipment, or sending staff for training—but does not sell anything to the New Zealand market. 

Without Section 54B, this company would be forced to absorb the 15% GST on all local expenses as a sunk cost. Section 54B acts as a solution: it allows the overseas business to register as a **"non-resident business claimant."** 

Essentially, this is a **"GST refund-only" registration**. The business does not charge GST to anyone (as it makes no local sales), but it can claim back the GST incurred on its New Zealand spending.

### The Eligibility Requirements

To qualify under Section 54B, a non-resident must meet strict criteria. 

| Requirement | Practical Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Genuine Business Operation** | You must be registered for a consumption tax (like VAT/GST) in your home country, or if your country lacks one, have worldwide taxable activities exceeding NZ$60,000 annually. |
| **Minimum Claim Threshold** | The expected input tax (GST refund) for the first taxable period after registration must be greater than **$500**. |
| **No Direct Supply to Unregistered Kiwis** | Your business activities must not involve providing services that will foreseeably be received in New Zealand by persons who are not GST-registered. |
| **No Taxable Supplies in NZ** | You must not be making, or intending to make, any taxable supplies in New Zealand. If you are selling locally, standard GST registration rules apply instead. |
| **No NZ GST Grouping** | You cannot be, or intend to become, part of a group of companies that makes taxable supplies in New Zealand. |

### Practical Examples

#### Scenario 1: Qualifying for Section 54B
**ZNA Airways** is an airline based in Singapore with no flights or customers in New Zealand. ZNA sends trainee pilots to a New Zealand aviation school, paying $10,000 for course fees (plus $1,500 GST) and $4,000 for accommodation (plus $600 GST).
* **Assessment:** ZNA is a genuine overseas business. Their NZ GST paid is $2,100 (exceeding the $500 minimum). They are not selling to New Zealanders, making local sales, or part of an NZ GST group. 
* **Outcome:** ZNA Airways can register under Section 54B and claim a $2,100 refund.

#### Scenario 2: Failing the Conditions
**Elite Tutoring USA** is a California-based company providing online tutoring to high school students living in Auckland.
* **Assessment:** While they are a genuine business, their service is received in New Zealand by people who are not registered for GST (the students). Furthermore, they *are* making taxable supplies in New Zealand (remote services to consumers).
* **Outcome:** Elite Tutoring USA **cannot** register under Section 54B. Instead, they must register under the standard rules for overseas businesses supplying remote services to NZ consumers and must charge GST on their tutoring fees.

### The Flip Side: Standard Registration for Overseas Sellers

If an overseas business *is* actively selling to New Zealand customers (and exceeds the $60,000 registration threshold), they fall under standard GST registration, not Section 54B.

In this standard scenario, the business operates much like a domestic entity:
1. **Output Tax:** They must charge 15% GST on sales to New Zealand consumers and pay this to Inland Revenue.
2. **Input Tax:** They can claim back the GST paid on New Zealand-based business expenses (e.g., local cloud hosting, local contractors) to the extent those expenses are used to make their taxable supplies.

Understanding the distinction between standard registration and Section 54B is critical for international businesses operating within or alongside the New Zealand economy.
